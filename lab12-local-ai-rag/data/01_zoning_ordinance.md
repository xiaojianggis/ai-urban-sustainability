# City of Elmwood Zoning Ordinance (Excerpts)

*Fictional document for teaching. Elmwood is not a real city.*
Adopted March 2024. Status: adopted.

## Article 1. Purpose

This ordinance regulates the use of land and the size and placement of buildings in the City of Elmwood in order to protect public health, encourage housing choice, support transit, and meet the climate goals of the Elmwood 2045 Comprehensive Plan.

## Article 4. Residential Districts

### Section 4.1 Districts established

Elmwood has three residential districts: R-1 Low-Density Residential, R-2 Two-Family Residential, and R-3 Multifamily Residential.

### Section 4.2 R-1 Low-Density Residential District

The R-1 district is intended for detached single-family homes on individual lots.

- Minimum lot size: 6,000 square feet.
- Maximum building height: 35 feet (2.5 stories).
- Front setback: 20 feet minimum.
- Side setback: 5 feet minimum on each side.
- Rear setback: 25 feet minimum.
- Maximum lot coverage: 40 percent.
- One accessory dwelling unit (ADU) is permitted per lot. See the ADU Regulations.

### Section 4.3 R-2 Two-Family Residential District

- Minimum lot size: 4,000 square feet.
- Maximum building height: 38 feet (3 stories).
- Duplexes and two-unit conversions are permitted by right.
- Maximum lot coverage: 50 percent.

### Section 4.4 R-3 Multifamily Residential District

- No minimum lot size.
- Maximum building height: 55 feet.
- Maximum floor-area ratio (FAR): 2.0.

## Article 5. Mixed-Use Districts

### Section 5.1 MU-T Mixed-Use Transit District

The MU-T district applies to land within one-half mile of the Elmwood Central and Riverside rail stations.

- Maximum building height: 75 feet.
- Maximum floor-area ratio (FAR): 4.0. A bonus FAR of 1.0 is available when at least 15 percent of units are affordable to households earning 60 percent of area median income.
- Ground-floor active uses (retail, services, lobbies) are required along streets designated as Main Streets.
- Parking maximums apply instead of minimums. See the Parking and Transportation Standards.

## Article 7. Short-Term Rentals

### Section 7.1 Where short-term rentals are allowed

A short-term rental is a dwelling, or part of a dwelling, rented for fewer than 30 consecutive nights. Short-term rentals are allowed in all residential and mixed-use districts, subject to the rules in this article.

### Section 7.2 Rules for short-term rentals

- The host must use the dwelling as their primary residence (at least 275 days per year).
- When the host is not present, the dwelling may be rented for no more than 90 nights per calendar year.
- An accessory dwelling unit may not be used as a short-term rental.
- A short-term rental permit is required. See the Permit and Review Process.
- Each listing must display the city permit number.

## Article 9. Nonconforming Uses

A building that was legal when built but does not meet current rules may remain. It may be repaired, but it may not be enlarged in a way that increases the nonconformity.
